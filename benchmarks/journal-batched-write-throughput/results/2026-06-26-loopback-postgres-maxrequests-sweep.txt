max-requests sweep at window=2ms (1k/2k/3k actors). Question: does raising batch.max-requests help throughput?
Answer: no - on loopback it HURTS, because large batches under-utilise batch.parallelism.

Conclusion
- max-requests=100 (the current default) is the best of the values tested; throughput falls monotonically as
  max-requests grows. window=2ms keeps full throughput (same as the earlier 3/5/10 ms runs at mr=100).
- Why: groupedWithin emits a batch when EITHER max-requests is reached OR the window elapses. With small max-requests a
  round of N concurrent writes becomes ceil(N/max-requests) batches that fan out across batch.parallelism (=8) and the
  connection pool. With large max-requests the whole round collapses into ONE big batch handled by ONE mapAsync slot -
  the other slots idle and the single large transaction is slower than many small pipelined ones. Effective parallelism
  ~ min(ceil(N/max-requests), parallelism), so big max-requests starves parallelism.
- Practical rule (loopback): keep max-requests roughly <= actorCount / parallelism so all slots stay busy; 100 is near
  optimal for 1k-3k actors at parallelism=8. The real throughput lever is parallelism (x pool size), NOT max-requests.
- Caveat: loopback has ~zero network RTT, which is why many small batches win (round-trips are almost free). On a
  NETWORKED database, fewer/larger batches reduce round-trips and the optimum max-requests will be higher - worth
  re-measuring there. Even so, very large values (5000+) also inflate transaction size and the failure-retry cost.
